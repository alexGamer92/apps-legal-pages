RAM Rush Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 18, 2026 [SET ON PUSH DAY]

RAM Rush (“RAM Rush: 8-Bit Memory Run” on the App Store) is an 8-bit iPhone platformer published by Alexandru Struta, an individual developer based in Romania (the “developer”, “we”, “us”). This policy explains what information the game handles, where it goes and what choices you have. It describes version 1.3 of the game. Contact: alexandrustruta@gmail.com.

1. Short version

  • There is no account and no sign-in. The game never asks for your name, email address or phone number.
  • Your progress is saved on your iPhone. We have no server of our own and your saved progress is not uploaded to us. If iCloud is on for the game, a copy is kept in your own iCloud account so a new iPhone can continue it; we cannot read it.
  • For players aged 16 and over, the game uses PostHog analytics: anonymous play events and crash reports, to improve the game. It never records your screen or your touches. Where Google's consent message is required (section 3), analytics is off. You can turn it off in Settings > ANALYTICS (section 11).
  • The only ads are optional rewarded ads served through Google AdMob, which may show an ad from Unity Ads: after losing all your lives you can choose to watch one for +1 life. Google and Unity receive information about your device and the ads you see so they can show ads, measure them and protect them from fraud (section 3).
  • Before the first ad, the game asks with Apple's tracking prompt (not to players under 13) whether Google and Unity may use your iPhone's advertising identifier (IDFA). Your answer changes nothing in the game.
  • ECC Mode, the optional subscription, is sold by Apple. We never see your payment details.

2. Information stored on your device

The game keeps your progress on your iPhone, in the app's own storage, so it can work:

  • your best results;
  • the levels you have cleared;
  • the run you are playing, so you can resume it;
  • your settings;
  • the age range iOS shares with the game, if any (section 10).

This information contains no name, email address or contact details, and these files are not sent to us (the analytics in section 11 send single events from play, not your saved progress). Deleting the app deletes it from the iPhone; the iCloud copy (below) stays until you remove it. Your iPhone's own backups (iCloud or computer) may include it, depending on your backup settings.

iCloud copy. If you are signed in to iCloud and iCloud is on for the game, a copy of your best results, cleared levels, golden chips found and weekly challenge results is kept in your own iCloud account (Apple's iCloud key-value storage), so a new iPhone signed in to the same account can continue your progress. The copy follows the iCloud account signed in on the iPhone: if another account signs in, the progress on this iPhone is added to that account's copy too. Your settings, the age range, the run you are playing and the week's challenge pick are not copied. The copy is stored by Apple under your account; it is not sent to us, Google or Unity, and we cannot read it.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob and Unity Ads)

Rewarded ads only, always your choice. RAM Rush shows ads through Google AdMob, a service of Google, using the Google Mobile Ads software. There are no banner ads and no ads that interrupt play. The only ad is offered after you lose all your lives: you may tap Watch ad: +1 life. Restart and Map never need an ad. ECC Mode players see the same optional offer. Ads are limited to content rated for teens (T).

What Google receives. When the game loads, requests or shows an ad, the Google Mobile Ads software in the app sends Google device and usage information, for example your IP address, your approximate location derived from your IP address, your device model, a device identifier that iOS gives each app developer (the identifier for vendor, IDFV, which is not the advertising identifier) and your interactions with ads. Google uses it to serve ads, measure them and protect against fraud and abuse. Google handles it under the Google Privacy Policy; How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services explains more. We do not receive this information from Google except as aggregate reports.

Unity Ads. Google's ad software uses Unity Ads as a second ad source. The Unity Ads software in the app starts together with Google's whenever ads are allowed, and it takes part in every ad request, bidding for the ad against Google. Unity therefore receives similar device and ad information, under the Unity Game Player and App User Privacy Policy, even when the ad shown comes from Google.

Apple's tracking prompt. Before the first ad request the game shows Apple's App Tracking Transparency prompt once (not to players under 13). If you allow it, the Google Mobile Ads and Unity Ads software can send Google and Unity your advertising identifier (IDFA) to personalize and measure ads; if you don't, it is not sent. Either way the Google Mobile Ads and Unity Ads software can send the identifier for vendor (IDFV) described above. You can change your answer in Settings > Privacy & Security > Tracking.

Consent in Europe. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent message (its User Messaging Platform) is shown before any ad is requested. Depending on your choice, Google may show non-personalized or limited ads. Your choice is also passed on to Unity. You can change your choice at any time with the Privacy choices button in the game.

When no ad can be shown. If you are offline or no ad is available, no ad plays and no extra life is given; nothing is sent to Google until the game can request an ad again.

4. Report an ad

If you see an ad that is inappropriate, misleading or broken, tap Report an ad in the game's Settings. It opens an email to us in your own Mail app; tell us what the ad showed, roughly when you saw it and, if you can, attach a screenshot. We use the report to block the ad or report it to Google or Unity, and your email only to handle it (section 6). Every ad also has its ad network's own options or information button (Google's or Unity's), where you can learn more about the ad or report it to that network directly.

5. ECC Mode purchases

ECC Mode is an optional weekly subscription sold through the App Store. Apple processes your payment, your Apple Account and your purchase history under the Apple Privacy Policy. We never see your card or bank details. The game asks Apple for the product's name and price and for your active subscription, and starts a purchase or a restore only when you tap the button.

6. Emails to us and these pages

If you email us, we receive your email address, your message and anything you attach. We use them only to answer you and fix problems. Please don't send passwords or payment details. Our email provider (Google) stores this correspondence for us.

These web pages have no cookies, analytics or tracking. They are hosted by Cloudflare, which processes standard request information, such as your IP address and browser details, to deliver and protect them.

7. Legal bases (EU/EEA and UK)

The developer, Alexandru Struta (Romania), is responsible for the information described here. Where the GDPR applies:

  • showing a rewarded ad you ask for relies on our legitimate interest in keeping the game free; personalized ads, and storing or reading information on your device for ads, rely on your consent in Google's consent message, which you can change at any time with Privacy choices;
  • selling and restoring ECC Mode is needed to provide what you buy (performance of a contract);
  • answering emails relies on our legitimate interest in supporting the game, or on steps you ask us to take;

We do not sell your information. PostHog stores our analytics in its EU region (section 11). Google, Unity and Apple may process information outside your country, including in the United States, under the safeguards described in their own policies. We may disclose information if the law requires it or to protect legal rights.

8. How long information is kept

  • Your progress stays on your iPhone until you delete the app. Deleting the app deletes it from the iPhone; the iCloud copy stays in your iCloud account. To stop the copy, turn off iCloud for RAM Rush in your iPhone's settings or delete the game; what is already in iCloud is managed in your iCloud settings. We cannot recover either. Device backups may keep copies.
  • Emails are kept as long as needed to handle your request and any follow-up, then deleted, unless the law requires us to keep them.
  • Analytics events and crash reports are kept by PostHog for up to one year and then deleted automatically.
  • Google, Unity and Apple keep their records under their own policies. Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription or delete Apple's records.

9. Your rights

Depending on where you live (for example under the GDPR in the EU or the CCPA in California), you may have the right to access, correct or delete information about you, to restrict or object to processing, to data portability and to withdraw consent. Email us to use these rights; we may need to confirm the request is yours. We do not hold your game data, ad data or purchase records, so for those we may refer you to Google, Unity or Apple. Our analytics (section 11) carry only a random identifier for each installation and no name or email, so we usually cannot tell which of it is yours; you can stop it at any time with Settings > ANALYTICS. You can also complain to a data protection authority, for example in the country where you live or, in Romania, the ANSPDCP (dataprotection.ro).

California and other US states. The information described in sections 3, 4 and 6 falls into these categories: identifiers (IP address, the identifier for vendor (IDFV), the advertising identifier (IDFA) if you allow tracking, an identifier the Unity Ads software assigns, a random identifier the PostHog software makes for each installation of the game, and your email address if you write to us), commercial information (Unity lists purchase history or purchase tendencies among the data its software collects), internet or other electronic network activity (device details, your interactions with ads and, through PostHog, events from your play in the game and crash reports) and geolocation data (only your approximate location, derived from your IP address). We do not collect sensitive personal information and we do not sell personal information. Our ad partners Google and Unity may use the identifiers and ad interactions above to choose ads based on your activity in other companies' apps and websites (the advertising identifier only if you allow tracking); some state laws may treat this as sharing personal information. We will not treat you differently for using any of these rights.

10. Children

RAM Rush is intended for players aged 13 and over. It is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, email us and we will delete it.

Age range. On iOS 26 and later, the game asks iOS for your age range, using Apple's Declared Age Range feature, when it first starts (and again later if no range was shared), before Google's consent message. You (or a parent, for a child's account) decide whether to share it, and the game receives only a range (under 13, 13 to 15, or 16 and over), never your birth date. The range is stored only on your iPhone and is never sent to us, Google, Unity or PostHog. It is used only to choose the type of ads Google may show, whether ECC Mode offers appear and whether analytics may run (only from 16, section 11), and with each ad request the game tells Google just the type of ads: for players under 13 Google applies its child-directed treatment and the game shows no ECC Mode offers; for players aged 13 to 15 Google applies its treatment for teens; for all players under 16 the game also tells Unity to show only non-personalized ads. If you don't share a range, or your iPhone runs an earlier iOS, the game plays as usual with ads limited to content rated for teens.

11. Analytics and crash reports (PostHog)

What is sent. From version 1.3 the game uses PostHog, a product analytics service, to understand how the game is played and to fix problems. When analytics is on, the PostHog software in the app sends PostHog:

  • play events, for example which level you started, cleared, restarted or failed, how much RAM you banked, whether you found the golden chip, your weekly challenge time and how a rewarded ad ended;
  • app events: installed, updated, opened and sent to the background;
  • crash and error reports.

The game does not record your screen and does not send the positions of your touches.

With each event the software sends a random identifier it makes for this installation of the game, a random session identifier, the app's version and build number, the name and version of the operating system, build details (the app's name and bundle ID, whether it is a TestFlight or test build, whether it runs in a simulator or on a Mac, and the version of the analytics software), your device manufacturer, model and type (for example phone or tablet), screen size (and, on a foldable iPhone, whether it is folded), language and time zone, whether you are on Wi-Fi or mobile data, and a few technical settings of the analytics software. It sends no name, email address or account (the game has none), no advertising identifier (IDFA), no identifier for vendor (IDFV) and no location. We do not link this information to you, and the random identifier changes if you delete and reinstall the game.

Why. To see which levels are too hard or too easy, to find and fix crashes and errors, and to improve the game.

Where. PostHog's EU cloud: eu.i.posthog.com receives the events, and eu-assets.i.posthog.com sends the game the analytics settings. PostHog receives your device's IP address with each request to either. For the events, the game tells PostHog not to work out a location from the IP address, and the game's PostHog project discards it instead of storing it. The settings request carries only the game's project key and the analytics software's version, and is handled under PostHog's privacy policy.

Who it applies to. Only players aged 16 and over, by the age range iOS shares (section 10). For players under 16, and when no age range is shared or the iPhone runs an iOS earlier than 26, analytics never runs. Where Google's consent message is required (section 3), analytics never runs, whatever you answer in that message: the message is about ads and does not name PostHog. Elsewhere it is on unless you turn it off.

Your choice. Settings > ANALYTICS (shown to players aged 16 and over) turns analytics off at once, and on again. Where Google's consent message is required, analytics stays off whatever this switch shows. Turning it off stops new events at once, but events already waiting on your iPhone (for example from a moment offline) may still be sent once afterwards.
